One room, above a print shop

Eleven members, a rack of mismatched plates, and a coach who wrote every program by hand.

2017

The ring room opens

A licensed corner coach joined, and boxing stopped being a Tuesday-night side project.

2019

The quiet room, for recovery

We stopped treating mobility as an afterthought and built a room just for it.

2022

Private coaching gets its own floor

1:1 members stopped sharing rack space with the open-gym crowd during peak hours.

2026

Eighteen bodies, capped, still

Bigger membership, same cap per session — the number that started this whole approach.

What actually runs the floor

Four beliefs. Flip each one.

Coached, not crowded

Tap to flip

Every block on your plan is written by the coach whose name is on it — and they're on the floor when you train it, not managing five rooms at once.

Small, on purpose

Tap to flip

We stop selling a session slot at eighteen bodies. It caps our revenue on paper and it's the one number we've never moved.

Maintained, not just mopped

Tap to flip

Plates are calibrated quarterly. Platforms get resurfaced before they show it. Rust is a repair ticket, not a personality trait of the gym.

The long game, always

Tap to flip

Four-week blocks, reviewed against your own numbers. We'd rather you stay seven years than hit a six-week transformation and quit.
